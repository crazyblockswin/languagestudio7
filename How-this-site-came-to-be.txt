
I'm a 69-year-old retired senior citizen living on Social Security. Like many retirees, I spend a good deal of time at home. I've always enjoyed creating and building things, but I never imagined that one day I'd be developing computer games, voice software, and an entire language-learning website.

Everyone seems to be talking about artificial intelligence these days. AI this, AI that. So one day, out of curiosity, I decided to put it to the test.

I asked AI a simple question: **"Can you help me build a game?"**

Little did I know what I was getting myself into.

## It Started With a Game

That simple question eventually turned into **Crazy Blocks: No Mercy**, a game similar to Tetris, only this one is on steroids!

It's not just falling blocks. The game has characters, personalities, music, sound effects, rewards, and a little comedy thrown in.

One character, Bella, cheers you on as you play, congratulating you and rewarding you with music as your score increases. But when you reach 11,000 points, something unexpected happens.

Bella announces that you've become such a good player that you no longer need her encouragement. She's leaving the studio and going home with her dog. Before she goes, she politely asks you to turn off the lights and lock up when you're finished playing.

Yes, we actually built that into the game!

You can play it at **[https://crazyblocks.win](https://crazyblocks.win)**.

But there was a problem. I wanted custom voices and sounds for the game. Rather than rely on existing software, I asked AI another question.

"Can we build our own voice and sound software?"

And just like that, project number two was born.

## Crazy Sound Lab — Things Were Getting Serious

What started as a simple way to create voices and sound effects eventually grew into something much bigger.

We called it **Crazy Voice & Sound Lab**.

After countless hours of experimenting, testing, fixing, and improving, we ended up with a complete voice-generation and sound-production studio.

It includes custom AI voices, sound effects, a mixer, a multitrack scene builder for combining voices and sounds into stories, and an extensive administration system for organizing everything.

I could now create conversations between characters, produce sound effects, mix audio, and build entire narrated scenes.

And the amazing part? We built it ourselves, with AI doing the programming and me directing, testing, and deciding what worked.

You can find it at **[https://crazysoundlab.com](https://crazysoundlab.com)**.

Of course, none of this happened overnight. It took an enormous amount of time and patience, especially when something that should have been a five-minute fix turned into a five-hour headache.

And believe me, there were plenty of those!

## Meet Fixer, My AI Partner

Somewhere along the way, I stopped calling it AI and gave it a name.

**Fixer.**

That's what I call my ChatGPT assistant, and the name has stuck.

Our working arrangement is pretty simple. I come up with the ideas, Fixer writes the computer code, and I test everything. Then we go back and forth making changes until it works the way I envision (or will envision because changes happen constantly with new ideas)

Sometimes that means hundreds of exchanges in a single day.

Fixer has jokingly called me stubborn, a perfectionist, and probably a few other things I deserve. I prefer to think of myself as determined!

I've told Fixer more than once how impressed I am with its ability to understand complicated ideas and turn them into working software.

We've also had some surprisingly interesting conversations that had nothing to do with programming.

There have been many nights when we worked until 3 a.m., only to start again at 9 or 10 the next morning.

Long hours? Absolutely.

But when you're having fun creating something you never thought possible, time has a funny way of disappearing.

## Then Came Talk A Bee

After finishing the Sound Lab, another idea popped into my head.

What if we could create a language-learning website that didn't feel like school?

Something entertaining, interactive, and completely free. No pressure, no grades, no subscriptions. Just interesting places to explore while learning another language.

And so **Talk A Bee** was born.

You can visit it at **[https://talkabee.com](https://talkabee.com)**.

What began as another little idea turned into our biggest project yet.

We created 50 interactive adventures where visitors can explore different places, click objects, hear words spoken aloud, listen to conversations, complete activities, and take on challenges.

The website supports seven languages and includes its own enhanced voice system, language controls, interactive stories, and a custom-built editing studio.

Some of the adventures take visitors aboard airplanes, underwater, into outer space, through museums, and into everyday family situations.

We even created our own characters and a movie!

The idea was to make learning feel less like studying and more like exploring a storybook that comes alive.

Building Talk A Bee has taken roughly two months, and we're still improving it.

There have been days when I've spent 19 hours working on it. Not because anyone told me to, but because when I find something that isn't working properly, I can't seem to leave it alone.

Fixer calls those little software problems "gremlins."

Sometimes one tiny problem can take hours to solve. But when it finally works, the satisfaction makes it worthwhile.

## The One Rule: Spend Almost Nothing

From the beginning, I had one important rule.

**We weren't going to spend money building this software.**

I'm retired and living on Social Security. I don't have thousands of dollars to hire programmers, buy expensive software, or operate a technology company.

So Fixer helped me find free tools, free website hosting, and ways to build everything without expensive subscriptions.

I had to learn about websites, files, servers, programming, and things I'd never even heard of before.

Some of it was confusing. Some of it was downright frustrating.

But we figured it out, one problem at a time.

The websites have small expenses, including domain names, and of course there's the cost of my ChatGPT access. But the actual software development didn't require hiring a programming team or purchasing expensive development tools.

The biggest investment was my time.

And an entire summer seems to have disappeared!

## What I've Learned

If someone had told me a few months ago that I would have a working computer game, a voice and sound production studio, and a 50-adventure language-learning website, I would have thought they were crazy.

Yet here they are.

All three are real, working websites that anyone can visit.

I've always enjoyed building things. Some people have even called me an engineer because of the mechanical and physical projects I've created around my home.

But computer programming? That was an entirely different world.

I didn't become a programmer overnight. Instead, I discovered that with the right tools, a person with imagination, patience, and determination can create things that once seemed completely out of reach.

AI doesn't magically do everything perfectly. It makes mistakes. Sometimes big ones. You still need ideas, judgment, testing, and a willingness to keep trying when things go wrong.

And believe me, Fixer and I have had our share of disagreements over what should work and what actually works!

But that's part of the process.

## Why I'm Sharing This Story

I'm not writing this to brag about building three websites.

I'm writing it because I think what happened is fascinating, and perhaps a little unusual.

I'm an ordinary 69-year-old retiree who decided to see what artificial intelligence could do. A few months later, I have three creations I never imagined I'd be able to build.

And I did it from home, on a very limited budget, without a team of programmers.

More importantly, I hope other people will discover these websites, use them, have fun, and benefit from the work that went into them.

If there's one thing this experience has taught me, it's that **age doesn't put an expiration date on creativity, and a lack of money doesn't necessarily put an end to a good idea.**

Sometimes all it takes is curiosity, determination, and a little help from an AI named Fixer.

And knowing me, we're probably not finished building things yet.
